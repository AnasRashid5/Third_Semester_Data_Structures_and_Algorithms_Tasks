Robotics is the interdisciplinary field combining mechanical engineering, electrical engineering, computer science, and control theory to design, build, program, and operate robots. Robots are programmable machines capable of carrying out complex actions automatically, augmenting or replacing human labor in physical tasks.

Robot kinematics describes the motion of robot links and joints without reference to the forces causing that motion. Forward kinematics computes the position and orientation of the end effector given joint angles. Inverse kinematics determines the joint angles required to place the end effector at a desired position and orientation, a problem that often has multiple solutions or no closed-form solution for complex manipulators.

Actuators convert electrical signals into physical motion. Direct current motors provide smooth continuous rotation and are widely used in wheeled robots and servo systems. Stepper motors rotate in discrete increments, enabling precise positioning without feedback. Hydraulic actuators deliver high force using pressurized fluid, common in heavy industrial robots. Pneumatic actuators use compressed air for fast and compliant motion.

Sensors provide robots with information about their own state and the surrounding environment. Encoders measure joint angles and rotational velocity. Inertial measurement units combine accelerometers and gyroscopes to estimate orientation and linear acceleration. Lidar emits laser pulses and measures return times to produce three-dimensional point clouds of surrounding geometry. Cameras capture rich visual information processed by computer vision algorithms.

Simultaneous localization and mapping, commonly called SLAM, allows a robot to build a map of an unknown environment while tracking its own location within that map. Occupancy grid maps represent free and occupied space in a discretized grid. Feature-based SLAM detects and tracks landmarks across sensor frames. Graph-based SLAM formulates the problem as a pose graph optimization minimizing the inconsistency between sensor observations and predicted measurements.

Path planning finds collision-free trajectories connecting start and goal configurations. Configuration space represents the robot's possible states, and obstacles in physical space project into forbidden regions of configuration space. The rapidly-exploring random tree algorithm samples random configurations and grows a tree toward them, quickly finding paths through high-dimensional spaces. The A-star algorithm finds optimal paths on discrete graphs using a heuristic estimate of remaining cost.

Robot control translates planned trajectories into actuator commands. Proportional-integral-derivative controllers compute actuator outputs from the error between desired and actual state, the integral of error, and the rate of change of error. Model predictive control optimizes a sequence of future actions over a receding horizon while respecting constraints on state and actuator limits.

Machine learning enables robots to acquire skills from experience rather than explicit programming. Imitation learning trains robots by observing human demonstrations. Reinforcement learning trains robots by rewarding successful task completion in simulated or real environments. Sim-to-real transfer addresses the gap between simulated training environments and the real world by domain randomization and careful physical modeling.

Human-robot interaction studies how people collaborate with robots safely and intuitively. Collaborative robots, called cobots, work alongside humans on assembly lines with force-limiting mechanisms that prevent injury on contact. Natural language interfaces allow workers to instruct robots verbally. Social robots interpret facial expressions and gestures to participate in human communication contexts.

Autonomous vehicles integrate robotics, computer vision, sensor fusion, and planning to navigate public roads without human intervention. Lidar, camera, radar, and ultrasonic sensors provide complementary views of the environment. High-definition maps encode precise road geometry and traffic rules. Behavior prediction models anticipate the actions of other vehicles and pedestrians to enable safe and efficient driving decisions.
