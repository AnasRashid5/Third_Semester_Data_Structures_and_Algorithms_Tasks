Natural language processing is a field of artificial intelligence that enables computers to understand, interpret, and generate human language. Text and speech are the most natural forms of human communication, and NLP bridges the gap between unstructured language and structured computation.

Tokenization splits raw text into units called tokens, which may be words, subwords, or characters depending on the chosen granularity. Whitespace and punctuation delimit word boundaries in most Latin-script languages, but languages like Chinese and Japanese require more sophisticated segmentation. Subword tokenization methods like byte-pair encoding handle rare words by splitting them into frequent subunit sequences.

Part-of-speech tagging assigns grammatical labels such as noun, verb, adjective, and preposition to each token. Parsing constructs syntactic trees that represent the hierarchical grammatical structure of sentences. Constituency parsing groups tokens into phrases. Dependency parsing identifies directed grammatical relationships between individual word pairs. These structural analyses support downstream tasks like information extraction.

Named entity recognition identifies and classifies mentions of real-world entities in text. People, organizations, locations, dates, monetary amounts, and other domain-specific entities are detected by sequence labeling models. Coreference resolution links multiple mentions that refer to the same entity, essential for understanding pronoun references and abbreviations in long documents.

Sentiment analysis determines the emotional tone expressed in text. Lexicon-based approaches assign polarity scores to individual words and aggregate them. Machine learning classifiers trained on labeled reviews and social media posts learn more nuanced sentiment signals. Aspect-based sentiment analysis additionally identifies the specific product feature or attribute toward which sentiment is directed.

Word embeddings represent words as dense real-valued vectors in a low-dimensional space. Word2Vec trains a shallow neural network to predict context words from a target word, producing vectors where semantically similar words cluster together. GloVe constructs embeddings by factorizing a word co-occurrence matrix. Embeddings transfer semantic knowledge learned from large corpora to downstream tasks with limited labeled data.

Language models assign probabilities to sequences of words. N-gram models estimate the probability of each word from the preceding N minus one words. Recurrent neural language models maintain a hidden state that summarizes all preceding context. Transformer language models use self-attention to relate every token to every other token in the sequence, enabling parallelization across long contexts.

Large language models trained on hundreds of billions of words from the internet, books, and code exhibit emergent capabilities including question answering, summarization, translation, code generation, and reasoning. Instruction tuning and reinforcement learning from human feedback align these models to produce helpful, harmless, and honest responses.

Machine translation automatically converts text from a source language to a target language. Statistical machine translation used phrase-based models estimated from parallel corpora. Neural machine translation encodes source sentences into continuous representations and decodes them into target sentences. Attention mechanisms allow the decoder to selectively focus on relevant source positions at each generation step.

Information retrieval, the task of finding relevant documents for a user query, has deep connections to NLP. Bag-of-words models represent documents by term frequency vectors. Inverse document frequency down-weights terms that appear in many documents. Dense retrieval encodes queries and documents as vectors and retrieves by nearest-neighbor search, outperforming sparse retrieval on semantically phrased queries.
